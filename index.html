From the introduction

11:47 p.m. Your thumb stops.

A fifteen-second video shows a stranger opening the door of a car you cannot afford. You don’t want the car. At least that’s what you would say out loud. But your thumb has stopped.

In fifteen seconds, a car you weren’t looking for has become a question: what if it were mine?

Same product.
Different feeling.

A countdown can turn “Do I want this?” into “What if I miss out?” The question changes before the price does.

This book uses the seven deadly sins to examine that shift. See how comparison, recognition, urgency, and other impulses show up in a feed, on a sales page, and at checkout.

In 1981, Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman showed how describing the same choice in terms of gains or losses can change preferences. It’s one way to see what an offer puts front and center.

Greed
